Buckinghamshire grammar distance history

Every Buckinghamshire grammar school publishes an "allocation profile" after National Offer Day each year, showing exactly which oversubscription rule decided the last place offered — and, where that rule was distance, exactly how far out that went. Four years of these profiles show something worth knowing before treating any single year's distance as a benchmark for your own odds: it moves, sometimes by a lot, in a single year.

The clearest example: The Royal Grammar School's distance cut-off was 4.63 miles in 2023 — then 13.52 miles in 2026, nearly tripling. John Hampden Grammar swung from 6.70 miles in 2023 to 15.86 miles in 2025, then pulled back to 11.49 the following year. A family who moved based on one year's published distance could easily find the goalposts somewhere else entirely by the time their own child applies.

Why it moves this much

A school's final distance depends on how many places are used up by higher-priority rules first — siblings, catchment, Pupil Premium/Free School Meals priority, staff children — before distance is reached at all. A single unusually large sibling cohort, a shift in how many catchment-area families apply that year, or a change to a Priority Area's size can move the remaining distance-allocated places substantially, even with no change to the school itself. This is the same underlying mechanism as a Published Admission Number (PAN) change at primary level — a small shift upstream in the process can produce a large, visible swing at the end of it.

What this means practically: a distance figure from any single year — including the current one — is a data point, not a guarantee. If you're weighing a move based on a specific school's published distance, treat the four-year range shown here as the realistic band of outcomes, not the most recent number alone.
