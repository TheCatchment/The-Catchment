How to read this page. Every school below has a published furthest distance offered for Reception — the distance from home to school of the last child let in under the distance criterion, each year the council publishes it. A shrinking distance means the school is getting harder to get into from further away; a growing distance means it's easing. Some schools filled every place through siblings, faith, or other priority criteria before distance was ever used as a tie-breaker — for those years we show that instead of a distance figure. Harrow measures in miles; Hillingdon measures in metres. We've kept each borough in its own units rather than converting, since a conversion error is exactly the kind of thing that misleads a parent checking their own address.
What the tags mean.
  • More competitive — the catchment radius has shrunk and/or applications have risen over the three years shown. Harder to get in from further away than three years ago.
  • Less competitive — the radius has widened and/or applications have fallen. Easier to get in from further away than three years ago.
  • Steady — bouncing within a narrow band with no clear direction across the three years.
  • Unpredictable — swings up and down significantly year to year; not reliable enough to call a trend either way.
  • Usually has room — consistently undersubscribed across all three years; a reasonably reliable fallback preference.

A Voluntary Aided Catholic school (Diocese of Westminster) — its own admission policy gives priority to baptised Catholic children resident in the Ruislip, Eastcote and Harefield parishes ahead of any distance measure, with reserved places for Eastcote (up to 14) and Harefield (up to 6) applicants specifically. Distance is only ever used as a tie-break within a priority group, so it never appears as a published "furthest offered" figure. What the three years do show clearly: spaces open on National Offer Day have shrunk from 6 in 2024 to effectively none by 2025 — worth knowing if you're applying on a non-Catholic or out-of-parish basis, since categories 8–9 (the ones a family outside the three parishes would fall into) have progressively less room each year.

Field End Junior, Newnham Junior and Coteford Junior School aren't shown — they take Year 3 transfers from their linked infant school rather than a Reception intake, so they don't appear in council Reception allocation data.

Data-quality flags — checked before you rely on these figures.
  • Hillingdon's 2026 allocation document labels its distance column "Kilometres," while 2023–2025 are all metres — the raw figures (e.g. 704, 3,134) only make sense as metres, not km. Treated here as metres, but not yet confirmed with Hillingdon Council directly.
  • Bourne Primary's 2024 furthest-distance figure conflicts between two of Hillingdon's own published documents (974.3m in the 2024 report vs. 2,799.6m in the following year's report, for the same school and year). Shown here as published; not resolved.
