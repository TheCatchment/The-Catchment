Buckinghamshire grammar schools

What HA4/HA5 families need to know about the 13 Buckinghamshire grammar schools, the residency rules that govern moving into the county, and where nearby families tend to relocate to.

There are no grammar schools in Harrow or Hillingdon, and the closest fully selective county system to Ruislip, Eastcote and Pinner is Buckinghamshire — a short drive west along the A40 or A413. Unlike the South West Herts consortium, Buckinghamshire doesn't reserve places for children living outside the county in the way some families assume. This page sets out how the system actually works, what relocating involves, and which Bucks towns HA4/HA5 families tend to end up in.

Three of the 13 — Dr Challoner's Grammar, Dr Challoner's High and Chesham Grammar — sit on the Metropolitan line and are commutable without moving; those three are covered in commute-specific detail on the main Grammar Routes page. This page is about the other ten, and about the county's admissions system as a whole.

Before you read further: admissions policies, cut-off scores and catchment/priority areas are set independently by each school and by Buckinghamshire Council, and they can change year to year. This page reflects our current understanding — always confirm details directly with Buckinghamshire Council's admissions team or the individual school before making a decision as significant as a house move.

How the Buckinghamshire system works

Buckinghamshire runs a single countywide entrance exam, the Secondary Transfer Test (STT — still commonly called "the 11+" locally), sat in September of Year 6. It covers Verbal Reasoning, Non-Verbal Reasoning, English and Maths, and produces an age-standardised score. A score of 121 or above makes a child "qualified" for a grammar school place — but qualifying is not the same as being offered one. Every year there are more qualified children than places, so schools then apply their own oversubscription criteria (siblings, distance, and priority for children eligible for Free School Meals/Pupil Premium) to decide who actually gets in. A higher score above 121 does not improve a child's chances — it's a pass/fail threshold, not a ranking.

Children who attend a Buckinghamshire state primary school are entered for the test automatically. Children at any other school — including everyone in HA4/HA5 — need to actively register during the window that opens in early May and closes in mid-June of Year 6, then travel to a Buckinghamshire test centre in September to sit it, since testing isn't offered locally.

The residency question: do you have to move before the offer, or before term starts?

This is the part families most often get wrong. Buckinghamshire Council is explicit that qualifying for a grammar school place does not guarantee one, even if you later prove you've moved into the county. Distance from the school is used as a tie-breaker among qualified children when a school is oversubscribed, so where you live in Buckinghamshire matters for your actual odds — not just whether you live there at all.

There's also a hard cut-off date, usually 1 September the year before entry, for the home address used in the first round of allocations. Moving after that date means your previous address is still used for that round — your new address only counts for the reallocation round and beyond. And moving in doesn't have to mean the family's forever home: what the council actually asks for is evidence of your child's main residence — things like a tenancy agreement or completed purchase, council tax registration, and utility bills in your name at the new address, all from before the cut-off.

Appeals for grammar places are rarely successful, even for children who scored well above 121 — so the honest framing for families is: the only reliable way in is to have a genuine, evidenced address inside the catchment area for the specific school you want, in place before the relevant deadline — not a late scramble after National Offer Day.

Renting vs. buying

Because it's residency that's being evidenced — not homeownership — a rental tenancy can, in principle, establish an address just as validly as a purchase, provided it's genuinely your child's main home and you can document it (tenancy agreement, council tax, utility bills, electoral roll) from before the address cut-off date. Some families do use a rental as a lower-commitment way to secure a catchment address for the application year.

A few things worth weighing before going down that route:

  • Distance still decides it among qualified children. A rental just inside a catchment boundary may still lose out on the tie-break to a family who lives closer, so "any address in Buckinghamshire" isn't the goal — the address needs to be genuinely competitive for the specific school.
  • Short-term/purely admissions-driven rentals attract scrutiny. Schools and councils have seen this pattern before and can ask for evidence that a family is genuinely resident, not using a temporary address of convenience. Weak or late-dated paperwork is a common reason families lose out at the tie-break stage.
  • A rental doesn't fix the commute problem it creates. If a parent still works in or near London, moving further out changes two commutes (yours and eventually your child's, if the school offer doesn't land where expected) — factor this in alongside the schooling decision.
  • It's a one-way bet if the score doesn't land at 121+. Because the test happens in September of Year 6, after most tenancy or purchase decisions would need to be finalised, families are often committing to the move before knowing whether their child has qualified at all.

Where to get access to the most grammars

If maximising choice — rather than proximity to HA4/HA5 — is the priority, two towns stand out: each puts three of the 13 schools within the same town, covering both a boys' and a girls' option.

Widest choice

High Wycombe

Royal Grammar School (boys) and John Hampden Grammar School (boys) sit alongside Wycombe High School (girls) — three schools, two boys' options, all within the town itself. The trade-off: it's the furthest of the realistic Bucks clusters from Ruislip/Eastcote, at 25+ minutes.

Widest choice, co-ed included

Aylesbury

Aylesbury Grammar School (boys), Aylesbury High School (girls) and Sir Henry Floyd Grammar School (co-ed) all sit within the town — the only cluster of three that includes a genuinely co-ed option alongside single-sex ones. Also one of the furthest moves from HA4/HA5.

The Amersham/Chesham cluster covered below is the closest three-school grouping to Ruislip/Eastcote by distance, but it's two boys'/girls' pairs plus one co-ed rather than a true three-way choice within a single town.

Where HA4/HA5 families tend to relocate

This isn't drawn from a formal survey — it's the pattern that falls out of simple geography and transport links. Ruislip and Eastcote sit right on Hillingdon's western edge, so the realistic Bucks towns are the ones in the south of the county, reachable via the A40/A413 or the Chiltern Main Line into London Marylebone (useful if a parent still commutes into the city). Aylesbury and Buckingham, in the north of the county, are a considerably bigger move — 25 miles or more — and far less common for families moving specifically from HA4/HA5.

Most common

Gerrards Cross & Chalfont St Peter

The shortest move from Ruislip/Eastcote — roughly 15–20 minutes by car — with a direct Chiltern Line service into Marylebone. Feeds most naturally into the Amersham/Little Chalfont schools (Dr Challoner's Grammar and High School) and Beaconsfield High.

Common

Amersham & Chesham

Slightly further out along the Metropolitan/Chiltern lines, and the natural catchment towns for Dr Challoner's Grammar, Dr Challoner's High and Chesham Grammar directly. Popular with families prioritising proximity to a specific one of these three schools over commute time.

Common

Beaconsfield

A further option along the same A40/M40 corridor, home to Beaconsfield High School (girls) and within striking distance of the Wycombe schools. Tends to suit families who've widened their search to "south Bucks" generally rather than one specific school.

Less common

High Wycombe & Marlow

A longer move (25+ minutes), but still done by families specifically targeting the Royal Grammar School, Wycombe High, John Hampden or Sir William Borlase's — usually because of a particular school's reputation or fit rather than proximity alone.

Uncommon

Aylesbury & Buckingham

Rarely chosen by families relocating specifically from HA4/HA5, given the distance — this is a genuinely different part of the county, better suited to families already living further northwest.
